# Cold Email Copywriting — Knowledge Base

> **Purpose:** A single, structured reference for an AI copywriter agent to write high-performing B2B cold outreach (email + LinkedIn).
> **Contents:** (1) Copywriting principles, (2) Template copies, (3) GEX swipe file copies.
> **How to use:** Treat sections 1–3 as non-negotiable constraints, section 4 as psychology to draw on, section 5 as the template library, section 6 as building blocks (words/phrases/CTAs), and section 7 as the final checklist.

---

## 0. Core Philosophy

- **The bar is low.** Most cold outreach is formulaic, forgettable, and templated. The whole game is standing out.
- **Volume era of outbound.** Buyers get exponentially more messages since AI/automation. If a message looks vaguely like the norm, it vanishes into the "ocean of sameness."
- **Pattern disruptors win.** Language that stands out and leaves an impression is the name of the game.
- **Outcomes, not mechanisms.** No one wakes up wanting "SEO" — they want more traffic. Lead with what the buyer cares about; let curiosity about the "how" build naturally.
- **The best message** reads like the sender researched the person for 10 minutes and wrote it by hand. Templates exist so you don't rebuild structure each time — spend the energy testing *value propositions*, not formatting.

## 1. Hard Rules (The 55 Cheat Codes)

### Formatting & Length
1. **Never use the em dash "—".** Use a regular hyphen or short sentences.
2. **Paragraphs = 2 lines max.** Highly digestible and scannable. Optimize for mobile.
3. **Whole email ≤ 80 words.**
4. **One question mark per email.** More overwhelms the eye and weakens the close.
5. **No exclamation marks. Ever.** Even "?" used with restraint. Less hype = more trust.
6. **No memes or GIFs.** They signal automation/gimmickry and kill B2B credibility.
7. **Launch straight into the first line.** Skip "Hi John, hope you're well." Start with signal.

### Language & Tone
8. **Write so a 10-year-old understands it.** No corporate/technical jargon.
9. **Avoid repeating language/descriptors.** Repetition = templated/mass-email feel. Vary phrasing.
10. **Be ruthless with words.** Cut everything that doesn't add value. "Noticed" > "I noticed."
11. **Every line must justify the next.** If a line can be cut without hurting the message, cut it.
12. **Don't over-polish.** AI messages sound too smooth. De-polish: break rhythm, list 2 things not 3, start with "And"/"But," drop a rough aside in (brackets), let tone shift. Scrappiness feels human.
13. **Use human words:** "basically," "a bit," "kinda," "thing."
14. **Use brackets to inject tone/warmth:** "(and yep, I know this is random)," "(full story's a bit wild)."
15. **Keep a neutral tone. Minimise praise/flattery.** Praising someone you don't know diminishes your status, signals insincerity, and reads as AI-generated. Use neutral observations: "saw you've opened the Brisbane office and rolled out the advisory stream. Big couple of months."
16. **Never use "impressive."** (Also avoid "cutting-edge," "innovative," "enhance," "game-changing," "groundbreaking.")
17. **Use relatable metaphors the buyer already thinks in.** "Like having your best AE cloned" > "like Kafka event stream duplications."
18. **Be specific.** Humans think in specifics, not generalities.
19. **Use less enthusiastic punctuation. Play it cool.**
20. **Sometimes de-capitalise job titles / company names** (sparingly) to feel more personal.

### Positioning & Content
21. **Don't mention your company.** It flips the buyer's brain from "human connecting" to "sales rep pitching."
22. **Don't state your specific service or mechanism** — focus on the impact/outcome. Avoid the "how."
23. **Frame value without talking about yourself.** Describe the painful status quo, hint at the shift others are making, let them infer the solution. "Some teams are switching to…" not "We help companies…"
24. **Use "this" instead of "our product."** "This" sounds like a movement being passed around; "our product" sounds like a pitch.
25. **Avoid ROI metrics and numbers.** Without context/evidence they hurt trust, dehumanise the message, and signal a marketing email.
26. **Target pains, not problems.** Pain = "campaigns not getting replies because our messaging is off." Problem = "poor sales." Focus on the felt pain.
27. **Core idea must be clear by the end** of the message.
28. **All phrasing must be uniquely generated per prospect.** No reused generic intros/filler across accounts.

### Personalization & Relevance
29. **Lead with immediate engagement / relevance.** Front-load personal data points into the opening sentence to optimise preview text and signal "this is for you."
30. **Make personal data points sound natural.** "Hi Penn, saw you head up SO" > "Hi Penn, saw you're the Chief Executive Officer at StackOptimise Ltd."
31. **Case studies must be relevant.** Explicitly acknowledge commonality: "We work with Monzo (also in fintech)."
32. **Stack signals for relevance.** One signal = reason to reach out. Two = reason to respond. Layer context (funding, new GTM leader, product expansion) to build a "why now" narrative.
33. **Avoid cliché lines for saturated triggers** ("Congrats on the fundraise," "Noticed you're hiring an SDR"). Frame subtly.

### Curiosity, Framing & Psychology
34. **Use pattern disruptors.** Don't be wacky — be unexpected. Reference a colleague, their mission statement, a bold subject hinting at a competitor.
35. **Use negative framing.** "Most teams haven't cracked X" builds intrigue.
36. **Use anti-triggers to disarm.** Flip a buyer signal into a reason NOT to be interested: "Saw you're hiring SDRs — assumed this wouldn't be a fit yet." Signals confidence, lowers resistance.
37. **Use "not sure if…"** to create curiosity and disarm. "Not sure if this applies to your side — but it's helping other hotels right now like The Marriott." Don't be presumptuous.
38. **Use contrast to highlight change.** "Most teams are doing X — a few are trying Y."
39. **Suggest they're already late** (subtly). "Others are already switching," "Saw a few competitors go early on this." Urgency without hype.
40. **Frame as a side-thought** when lacking a strong signal. "Flagged this after seeing your product launch yesterday," "This came up in a Slack convo today."

### CTA & Offer
41. **Make the CTA low-commitment but specific.** "Can I share a 2-minute video explaining why hotels specifically are switching to this?"
42. **De-risk the mental lift.** "Not trying to change your stack — this just sits on top of it." / "Literally only takes 2 hours to set up."
43. **Offer a trio of hyper-personalised ideas.** "Can share 3 campaign angles we'd actually test for you."
44. **Be clear you *can*, not that you *have*.** "Can share 3 campaign ideas we'd run if we were in your shoes" > "already mapped 3 ideas" (which feels disingenuous if untrue). Honesty builds trust.

### Subject Lines
45. **Write the subject line last.** Let the body guide the hook. Distil the most interesting angle — not the company name.
46. **Make subject lines look internal.** Ditch polish/capitalisation. Mimic internal threads: "john & sales team." Blend into inbox noise.
47. **Subject and body must align.** If not, trust evaporates.

### Follow-Ups
48. **Follow-ups must always add a new angle.** No bumping. No "just checking in." Share new social proof, a different pain point, a new trigger.
49. **Follow-ups must flow / build narrative continuity.** "Last time I mentioned X — thought I'd expand." Don't let them feel like disconnected fragments.
50. **Use disarming language in follow-ups** to avoid annoying people (which burns accounts / triggers spam).
51. **Maintain email cohesion (the fluency effect).** Every line should flow — opener to CTA. A personalised opener + generic body breaks trust. Carry tone, voice, and narrative consistently across the sequence.

### Other
52. **Ditch filler phrases:** "just reaching out," "wanted to introduce," "touch base."
53. **Optimise for forwardability** (esp. enterprise — committees buy). "Might be useful for someone in product," "Not sure if this is more relevant to Emily?"
54. **Run the self-interest check.** Before sending: "Would I reply to this if I were them?" Every line should serve *their* agenda, not the sender's.
55. **Stack subject-line + disarm.** Hyper-specific personalised subject, then immediately disarm in the opener or P.S.

## 2. The 13 Rules of Engagement (Psychology)

Use as a checklist — but **pick 2–3 per message and lean in hard.** Don't cram all 13 into one email.

| # | Principle | What it means | How to apply |
|---|-----------|---------------|--------------|
| 1 | **Relevance** ("this is about ME") | Prospect should instantly think the message was crafted for them | Weave in role, time in role, industry, company, colleagues, product, pains, goals, tech stack, recent wins. Front-load into the opener. |
| 2 | **Curiosity** (power of mystery) | Humans hate information gaps (Zeigarnik effect) | Drop enough breadcrumbs to intrigue while positioning the solution enough to filter tire-kickers. |
| 3 | **Social Proof** (show legitimacy) | Cold = automatic skepticism | Reference case studies with competitors, adjacent-space companies, or recognisable brands. Never irrelevant/unknown ones. |
| 4 | **Authority Bias** (flex expertise) | Articulate their pain better than they can → they assume you have the solution | Get specific on buyer challenges + industry insights. Don't conflict with autonomy (rule 13). |
| 5 | **FOMO / Loss Aversion** (fear factor) | Losses hurt more than equivalent gains | Hint at opportunities others (ideally competitors) are already seizing. Subtlety over desperation. |
| 6 | **Reciprocity** (give first) | Effort/value creates obligation to respond | Personalise heavily (shows homework) or offer a free valuable resource (lead magnet). |
| 7 | **Clarity** (crystal-clear comms) | "Confuse, you lose." | Simple, scannable. No feature-talk or jargon. |
| 8 | **Urgency** (time factor) | Tie urgency to real business impact/market movements | Use time-sensitive triggers (active job roles, funding, milestones) to build a "why now" narrative. |
| 9 | **Ego** (make them the hero) | Everyone wants to look good and be ahead | (A) Subtle, relevant flattery — neutral tone. (B) Creative storytelling casting them as the leader. |
| 10 | **Pattern Interrupt** (be different) | Brain ignores the predictable | Be different in tone, format, or offer. Major theme of the whole playbook. |
| 11 | **Simplicity Bias** (easy does it) | Easier = more likely to engage | Make the CTA/ask effortless. |
| 12 | **Narrative Transportation** (story time) | Barriers drop when info comes as a story | Run a narrative through the sequence. Stories are emotive and build connection. |
| 13 | **Autonomy Bias** (let them choose) | People resist being forced | Use humbling disclaimers: "I suspect you've got everything under control." Give them the choice. |

## 3. The 4 Elements of the B2B Buyer's Brain

1. **Skepticism = default mode.** The buyer starts at a flat "no." Their brain protects time, budget, and reputation. Disarm fast.
2. **Attention Economics.** Hundreds of messages/ads/priorities compete. The brain filters obvious sales emails, long messages, and generic strategies. A few seconds to break the pattern.
3. **The Trust Paradox.** Buyers want to be sold to but hate being sold to. They want trends, competitor insights, and things that make them look good — but walls go up at the smell of a pitch. Be clear on the offer, human in the tone.
4. **Information Processing Patterns.** The buyer's brain runs this sequence:
   1. Who is this and can I trust them? → *elegant, accurate, specific personalisation*
   2. Is this relevant to me right now? → *personalisation + timing*
   3. What's the ask, effort, and risk? → *simple, low-friction, low-risk CTA*

### 3 Tactical Concepts
- **Message Market Fit (MMF):** Test many templates, hone in on the winner — but never stop testing. Keeps you out of spam (filters hate repetition) and fights copy fatigue.
- **Sequence Momentum:** No "just checking in." Each message builds on the last, injecting new value / fresh angles / different social proof. The compound effect drives action.
- **Forget The Rules:** Some top emails break "best practices" strategically — double case studies (body + P.S.), longer-but-justified emails, casual WhatsApp-style copy, CTA stacking (light ask in body, softer nudge in P.S.).

## 4. Template Library (with copies)

Each template includes structure and an example. Adapt to the audience.

### 4.1 Poke the Bear
*A single powerful question forcing the prospect to confront a hidden problem.*
```
{{FirstName}} - ever considered the impact of {{specific problem}} from {{current solution/situation}} causing {{negative outcome}}?
```
**Example:**
```
Penn - ever considered the impact of all the leads you might be missing from Smartlead failing to tag them?
```
**Use for:** regulated industries (healthcare/finance), consumer-facing brands, stagnant markets. **Tip:** A/B test a soft pain line: "I could be wrong, but what this could mean is {{assumed pain}}."

### 4.2 Storytelling
*Paint a vivid, emotive picture of what could be. Prospect = hero. No hard CTA.*
```
{{FirstName}}, picture this…
{{Set the scene with specific details}}
{{Normal day starts}}
But something's different.
{{Build through series of wins}}
{{Team/colleague reactions}}
{{Industry impact}}
{{Peak moment of success}}
(Okay, maybe got carried away there)
But that's exactly what we do for {{their industry}} through {{your solution}}.
{{Ultra-soft CTA}}
```
**Example:**
```
John, picture this…
It's a crisp London morning. You're waiting for your coffee in Pret, scrolling through social media.
New Naturecan campaign just went live.
But something's different.
Your phone is blowing up with notifications.
Engagement is through the roof. Genevieve (your sales director) can't believe it.
Your CBD products are flying off the shelf.
Word's got out to Charlotte's Web and CBDistillery...they missed a trick.
You'll think about this moment every time someone asks: how the hell did you build the most trusted organic wellness brand in the natural health industry?
(Okay, maybe a tad exaggerated).
But that's the kinda mornings I create for awesome brands, through outreach campaigns that make your customers actually feel something, for once.
When the time's right, I'd love your brand to be our next dance partner.
```
**Use for:** high-competition markets (recruitment/growth agencies), visionary leaders in scale-ups (50–500 employees). **Tip:** short snappy opening sentences + sensory detail ("crisp morning," "phone blowing up with notifications").

### 4.3 Cryptic Idea
*Tease an idea too good to ignore.*
```
Hi [FirstName],
I've got something for you. I gather [company] [what they do — show research].
[Genuine observation]. This morning, my Co-Founder and I came up with a [adjective] idea to help you [achieve specific goal] that'll [benefit].
Would it be alright if I forward the idea to you?
Thanks,
P.S [Context about your company with social proof]
```
**Example:**
```
Hi John,
I've got something for you.
I gather Nike's doubling down on DTC sales (always exciting when brands take control of their destiny, nice work with the SNKRS app). This morning, my Co-Founder and I came up with a wacky idea to help you attract more sneakerheads looking for exclusive drops.
Would it be alright if I forward the idea to you?
Thanks,
P.S. (For context, we help brands like Adidas and OnRunning generate leads through cold outreach campaigns that actually make people feel something).
```
**Use for:** e-commerce/retail, VPs of Marketing/Sales in growth mode. **Tip:** avoid gimmick words ("groundbreaking," "game-changing"). Keep it natural.

### 4.4 Creative Idea
*Position as a strategic partner by offering 3 fresh ideas.*
```
Hey {{first_name}} – just scanned your site & LinkedIn page.
I gather you help {{AI generated mission}} and I had some ideas for you. Given much thought to this kinda stuff?
{{Idea 1}}
{{Idea 2}}
{{Idea 3}}
The reason I ask is because we help companies {{insert how you help}}. Would it be useful to discuss executing any of these ideas?
```
**Example:**
```
Hey Alex – just scanned your site & LinkedIn page. I gather you help SMEs build up their data pipeline, so I cobbled together some ideas for you:
- Scraping job posts to identify companies ramping up their data teams (warmest ICP signals).
- Targeting buyers that follow your competitors on LinkedIn, like Mixpanel.
- Automating the process of mapping out existing data teams for key accounts.
We help consultancies like yours build out all this kind of AI-powered stuff to drive their outbound process.
Would it be useful to share how you can execute on these ideas?
```
**Use for:** broadly applicable; Seed/Series A founders. **Tip:** make the ideas genuinely valuable — ask "would I find these useful?"

### 4.5 Classic Twist
*Standard problem-solution-proof structure, sharpened.*
```
Hi {{FirstName}}, just checked out your {{specific data point}} - noticed {{observation with disclaimer}}.
I'm tentatively assuming {{pain point tied to strategic goal}}?
I work with {{similar companies}} to {{clear value prop}}.
In fact, we recently helped {{relevant company}} {{specific result}} without {{trade-off}}.
{{Low-friction CTA}}?
```
**Example:**
```
Hi John, just checked out your org chart - noticed you're lacking SDRs (LinkedIn data, grain of salt).
I'm tentatively assuming, therefore, you're juggling prospecting, running demos and closing deals all on your own - might be tough to manage as you scale across Europe?
I work with early-stage companies like Vertibox to deliver the impact of 5 SDRs for the cost of 1. In fact, we recently helped Personifics (another HR platform) increase monthly meetings without adding headcount.
With your permission, could I share some more info?
```
**Use for:** competitive sectors (HR tech, MarTech), consultants/agencies. **Tip:** hedge observations with humbling disclaimers ("grain of salt," "tentatively assuming").

### 4.6 Classic Twist Mini
*Slimmed-down version.*
```
Hi {{FirstName}},
{{quick recognition of achievement/situation}}.
I just {{research action}} on your {{specific area}} and noticed {{number}} things that could help you {{achieve goal}}.
I shot a personal video for you – is this the best place to drop it?
P.S. This same strategy helped {{competitor}} {{specific result}}.
```
**Example:**
```
Hi Austin, crushing it in the sportswear space.
I just reviewed your email infra (nike.com) and noticed 3 things that could help you drive reply rates.
I shot a personal video for you – is this the best place to drop it?
P.S. This same strategy helped Adidas lift their replies.
```
**Use for:** companies celebrating wins; when video is part of outreach. **Tip:** keep it snappy — brevity is the magic.

### 4.7 ABC CTA
*Give options, gamify, get engagement.*

**Value Bomb Edition:**
```
{{FirstName}} - {{context about their situation}}.
Take your pick:
A) {{High-value resource 1}}
B) {{High-value resource 2}}
C) {{High-value resource 3}}
Just reply with the letter.
```
**Example:**
```
John - as you're just 3 months into the role as VP, I'm here to help you out.
Take your pick:
A) 500 verified leads for your ICP (CTO/VP of tech at SaaS)
B) 3 juicy strategies to identify who's in market for HR software
C) A killer, high-converting outreach template specifically for Syncflow
Just reply with the letter.
```
**Qualification Edition:**
```
{{FirstName}} - choose your adventure:
If you're happy with {{current situation}} → Delete this email
If you want to know {{compelling result}} → Keep reading
If {{other person}} might be better positioned → forward this
If you've made it here → Reply {{word}} for a surprise
```
**Example:**
```
John - choose your adventure:
If you're happy with your sales pipeline → Delete this email
If you want to know how we boosted Rumble's lead volume → Keep reading
If Simon might be better positioned to handle this → Would really appreciate a forward
If you've made it here → Reply "adventure" for a surprise
```
**Use for:** multiple lead magnets/resources; early-stage startups; traditionally-dismissive industries (recruitment, lead-gen, cybersecurity).

### 4.8 Unhinged
*Break every professional-playbook rule — but back it up.*
```
{{FirstName}}, {{specific observation about their wins}}.
Give me a shout if you're looking to {{achieve similar outcome}} – we're using some {{casual description of approach}} that are {{bold claim}} for {{social proof}}.
Best,
{{Your name}}, A.K.A. {{outrageous but partially credible claim}} ({{specific credential}})
```
**Example:**
```
Emily, pretty sweet case studies you've secured with Electronic Arts, NatWest Group and The LEGO Group.
Give me a shout if you're looking to land similar logos – we're using some nifty tactics that are securing a sh*t ton of meetings for a couple of similar SaaS businesses also selling to Employer Brand teams.
Best, {{Your name}}, A.K.A. the best cold outbound-er in the world (1 of just 4 people globally to be a certified Clay and Smartlead expert)
```
**Use for:** bold/no-BS prospects (growth hackers, marketing/sales leaders, startups); when you have serious credentials; late in a sequence. **Tip:** balance the tone — too far = obnoxious, too safe = loses edge.

### 4.9 Direct Report
*Target the team, win the leader.*
```
Subject: For {{direct report #1}} and {{direct report #2}}
{{name}} - thought they might find this helpful given {{they're new...}}.
Typically, {{direct report title}} experience {{problem}} because {{flaw with status quo}}.
We fix this by {{solution}}. Used by teams like {{social proof}}. Does this resonate?
Thanks
```
**Example:**
```
Subject: For Sarah Chen and Tom Martinez
John - thought they might find this helpful given they're new to the sales team.
Typically, SDRs experience low response rates because they're using the same templated outreach as everyone else.
We fix this by providing personalised, pattern-breaking cold email frameworks. Used by teams like Gong and Salesforce.
Mind if I share how I'd target your buyers?
```
**Use for:** mid/large enterprises (200+ employees); new hires / teams in transition. **Tip:** naming direct reports in the subject boosts opens.

### 4.10 Short but Sweet
```
Hey {{FirstName}} – shortest email you'll receive all day. If someone could help {{insert how you help}}, would that be useful?
```
**Example:**
```
Hey John – shortest email you'll receive all day. If someone could help you identify CTOs/VPs in tech that are actually in-market buyers (for HR software), and don't lose them to competitors (like Personio & Letterbee), would that be useful?
```
**Use for:** simple high-level solutions; demand-capture (recruitment, refurbishment). **Tip:** success depends entirely on the strength of the offer.

### 4.11 Lead Magnet
*Lead with value, then ask.*
```
Hey {{FirstName}} – saw {{something relevant}}.
We help companies {{achieve results}}.
But I'd never expect you to take my word for it.
So I made you {{resource}} – if it's useful, let me know and maybe we chat?
```
**Example:**
```
Hey John – any luck with the SDR roles?
We help you ramp reps a lot faster than usual and book more meetings in their first 60 days.
But I'd never expect you to take my word for it.
So I made you a 3-step onboarding framework specifically for SDRs – if it's useful, let me know and maybe we chat?
```
**Use for:** early-stage startups (no logos yet); skeptical markets. **Tip:** personalise the magnet — a custom lead list / strategy plan beats a generic ebook.

### 4.12 Genius or Terrible
```
Subject: This is either genius or terrible
{first name}, There are 2 types of people:
1. Those who handle {pain point} at {company}
2. Those who don't
If you're #2, sorry for the inbox clutter - perhaps more relevant for {colleague}?
If you're #1, check this out:
{competitor} → Used our solution
{competitor} → +{result} {key metric}
{competitor} → {timeframe}
You → Reading this email
You → Curious (probably)
You → Next?
Send a "→" if you want the details.
```
**Example:**
```
Subject: This is either genius or terrible
John, There are 2 types of sales leaders:
1. Those who actively optimise their outbound pipeline.
2. Those who don't.
If you're #2, sorry for the inbox clutter. If you're #1, check this out:
Acme Corp → Used our playbook
Beta Inc → higher cold email reply rates
Delta Co → 30 days
You → Reading this email
You → Curious (probably)
You → Next?
Send a "→" if you want the details.
```
**Use for:** competitor-rich industries (SaaS, FinTech, eCommerce); fast-swap solutions. **Tip:** pair with a highly relevant competitor; if none, use an industry benchmark. *(Note: this template leans on metrics — deviates from Rule 25; test carefully.)*

### 4.13 The Internal Nudge
```
Hey {{FirstName}}, have you and {{Senior Colleague}} touched base about {{thing you help with}}? We're partnering with companies like {{Company}} to secure {{Outcome}} in just {{Timeframe}} - all without {{USP}}. Is this something {{Company}} is focusing on?
```
**Example:**
```
Hey Jake – any convos internally between you and Sarah (your RevOps lead) re: reducing SDR ramp time? We're helping teams like Gong ramp new hires faster without burning managers' time on manual coaching. Is this even a concern over at Acme?
```
**Use for:** RevOps leaders, sales managers, marketing/ops execs; 100+ headcount. **Tip:** reference a likely-real conversation (based on triggers), framed as an assumption.

### 4.14 Subject Line Message
```
Subject: Would you like to chat re: {{outcome}}
Hey {{FirstName}} – question in the subject line. Let me know if you'd like to connect.
```
**Example:**
```
Subject: Are your reps Dan & Josie struggling with email reply rates?
Hey Jake – popped the question in the subject line. Let me know if you'd like to connect.
```
**Use for:** limited deliverability; re-engaging cold prospects. **Tip:** add an analogy P.S.

### 4.15 Problem Potion
*Multiple specific pains side by side.*
```
Hi {{FirstName}}, been speaking to a handful of {{job title}} recently. {{Line linking the problems, e.g. "wondering if any of these hit home"}}
{{Problem 1}}
{{Problem 2}}
{{Problem 3}}
Any of these concerns atm?
```
**Example:**
```
Hi John, I've been speaking to a handful of VPs recently. Seems a lot of them are juggling similar headaches - wondering if any of these ring a bell for you…
- Your SDRs, Dan & Jake, are suffering from low reply rates.
- CEO Simon's on your case to implement more AI into your sales process.
- You've heard about Clay but have no idea where to start.
Any of these concerns atm?
```
**Use for:** early-stage founders; VP Sales/Marketing. **Tip:** each bullet crisp and ultra-specific (name a team member or tool).

### 4.16 Direct Pitch
*Cuts straight to the chase.*
```
Hi {{FirstName}}, any need for {{your solution/service}} at the moment?
{{Industry trend/opportunity}} is evolving so fast - nearly impossible for a {{prospect role}} to keep tabs on everything.
Up for a quick chat on how other {{similar prospects}} are staying ahead?
No worries if you're all set.
```
**Example:**
```
Hi John, any need for outbound support at the moment?
New sales tools like Clay and advanced AI models are popping up every other week. Almost impossible for VPs like you to keep up.
Fancy a quick chat on how others are staying ahead?
No worries if you're all good.
```
**Use for:** hot prospects with strong triggers; PMF testing new segments. **Tip:** front-load the "Any need for X?" question.

### 4.17 Mind-Benders
Three micro-variants that challenge assumptions:

**The Magic Trick:**
```
Hey {{FirstName}}, We discovered something odd. Turns out {{insight}} actually {{contrary to belief}}. Wanna see the trick behind it?
```
**The Conspiracy:**
```
Hey {{FirstName}}, Most {{role}} think {{assumption}}. But the numbers say they're wrong. Want proof?
```
**The One Sentence Bomb:**
```
Hey {{FirstName}}, Most {{practice}} fail because they {{reason}}.
Top 3 triggers that indicate {{persona}} might be in-market for {{solution}}:
{{Trigger 1}} / {{Trigger 2}} / {{Trigger 3}}
Leveraging any of these, or want to see how?
```
**Use for:** data/insight-heavy solutions; traditional industries & sophisticated buyers. **Tip:** keep razor-sharp — disclaimers dull the effect.

### 4.18 Permission-Based
```
Hi {{FirstName}} – permission to pitch and disappear forever if I don't hear back? (Guaranteed to be the most {{superlative}} pitch ever).
```
**Example:**
```
John – permission to pitch and vanish if I don't hear back? (Guaranteed to be the most personalised pitch you'll see all week).
```
**Use for:** hail-mary outreach; tough industries (recruitment, cybersecurity). **Tip:** add a little context so you attract real prospects, not tire-kickers.

### 4.19 Industry Insight
```
Hi {{FirstName}}, We {{what you do at volume/across industries}} - {{credibility statement}}. Here are {{number}} insights on what's working in {{year}}:
{{Insight 1}} … {{Insight N}}
Curious if you'd like a {{valuable resource}} that digs deeper into these for {{their industry}}?
```
**Example:**
```
Hi John, We send out a high volume of emails per month, targeting 15+ industries. Here's what's working right now:
- One-sentence emails with just one poke-the-bear question.
- Short, single-sentence paragraphs. Ultra-digestible formatting.

Happy to share a killer outreach template, based on these principles, tailored to coaching companies like Phoenix?
```
**Use for:** market-wide insights; thought-leadership plays; skeptical audiences (cybersecurity, finance). **Tip:** tie each insight to a known pain in their space.

## 6. Building Blocks (Components)

### 6.1 Power Words (swap corporate → human)
Use to de-polish and add human texture. **Adjectives:** nifty, bonkers, juicy, funky, badass, rad, killer, sweet, savage, wild, quirky, wacky, epic, legit, slick, snappy, cracking, top-notch, insanely effective, smashing, wicked, dead simple, spot-on. **Nouns:** whiz, mojo, secret-sauce, goldmine, superpower, blueprint, powerhouse, jackpot, breakthrough, playbook, rando, faff, graft, doddle. **Verbs:** crushing, vibing, jamming, noodling, dabbling, tinkering, hustling, nerding out, skyrocketing, cranking, brewing, rocking, grafting, chinwag, crack on, whack it over, lob it over. **Adverbs:** insanely, wickedly, ridiculously, majorly, genuinely, basically, honestly, properly, dead, massively, bang on, a bit of a, slightly.

### 6.2 Human Phrases (categories)

- **Transitions:** "On that note…," "It hit me the other day," "Here's the kicker…," "And here's where it gets interesting…," "Lo and behold," "Now for the fun part," "While I've got you," "Side note."
- **Casual closes:** "Fancy a chinwag?," "Worth a quick natter?," "Ball's in your court," "No rush, just a thought," "Let me know if it's your cup of tea," "I'll leave it with you," "When the time's right," "If this rings any bells," "Worth a shot," "No worries if not," "Food for thought," "Suspect you've got everything under control."
- **Engagement hooks:** "Quick favour…," "Not sure if this is relevant, but…," "You might have a better take on this…," "Ever thought about this?," "Tell me if I'm way off here," "Would love your two pence on this," "This reminded me of you," "Don't quote me on this but," "Real talk," "Heads up."

### 6.3 Phrases to AVOID
- **Generic openers:** "Hope you're well," "Hope this finds you well," "Just checking in," "I wanted to reach out," "I noticed that," "I saw that."
- **Overused adjectives:** impressive, innovative, cutting-edge, industry-leading, best-in-class, world-class, revolutionary, game-changing, next-generation, robust, seamless, state-of-the-art, unique, powerful, advanced.
- **Tired jargon:** leverage, optimise, streamline, synergy, scale, pain points, value proposition, best practices, circle back, touch base, low-hanging fruit, move the needle, hit the ground running.
- **Desperate follow-ups:** "Just following up," "Bumping this to the top," "Circling back," "Following up on my last email," "Friendly reminder."
- **Cliché social proof:** "Trusted by," "Used by," "Leading companies," "Fortune 500," "Global brands," "Industry leaders."
- **Pushy sales language:** "Limited time offer," "Don't miss out," "Act now," "Limited spots available," "Special offer," "Exclusive deal," "Time-sensitive."
- **Vague claims:** "10x your results," "Skyrocket your growth," "Massive results," "Transform your business," "Guaranteed success."
- **Overused trigger phrases:** "Saw you're hiring," "Noticed your recent funding," "Congratulations on the growth," "Saw your LinkedIn post," "Love what you're doing with."
- **Meeting requests:** "Jump on a quick call," "Grab 15 minutes," "Quick sync," "Hop on a call."

### 6.4 Subject Lines
Two opposite approaches, both valid:
- **The Unsexy (internal):** blend into inbox — "[FirstName], quick idea," "[FirstName], this morning," "Sales team update," "Q1 planning," "Regarding pipeline," "[FirstName] - Approval needed."
- **The Sexy (over-the-top intrigue):** "[FirstName], the next [IndustryLeader]?," "[FirstName] vs. [Competitor] - who wins?," "[FirstName], your purple cow."
- **Universal laws:** ≤6 words; avoid selling (unless the sexy approach); use their name/company; match tone to body (no bait-and-switch unless you disarm); **NEVER use "Re:" or "Fwd:"** (instant trust loss).

### 6.5 Follow-Ups
- **How many:** 2–3 is the sweet spot. Beyond 3 feels desperate and harms domain reputation. Small TAM → longer sequences OK; large TAM → keep lean (1–2), then move on.
- **The 3D Follow-Up Formula** — every follow-up must be:
  - **Different:** new angle, insight, or info.
  - **Disarming:** light, confident, easy to engage; self-deprecating humour helps.
  - **Direct:** cut fluff; concise and sharp (bend grammar if it keeps meaning).
- **9 creative follow-up angles:** one-liner question, lead-magnet offer, industry insight, value-prop extension, colleague reference + custom ideas, competitor reference (share intel), social-proof hook (client quote), last-employer connection, phone-number nudge.

### 6.6 Personalisation Points
Dig up the details that humanise and distinguish. **Personalisation stacking** = layering multiple relevant data points into one message/sentence to feel researched without feeling forced.
> Generic: "Noticed you're the VP of Sales at AcmeCorp - figured I'd reach out."
> Stacked: "Given you lead sales at AcmeCorp (focusing on AI-driven outbound), I suspect a lot of your time goes into ensuring SDR efficiency."

**Available data points** (scraped or AI-generated): company mission, title + time in role, LinkedIn recommendation, LinkedIn post, previous company, company analogy, scraped reviews (Google/Yelp/G2), case-study mention, web traffic (SimilarWeb), average order value, Google Organic rank, Google/LinkedIn/Facebook ads, tech stack (BuiltWith), "people also viewed," new promotion/job, open jobs + job-description keyword analysis, company news, leadership hire, competitor comparison, funding round, feature release, CRM/tech migration, expansion into new market, employee-retention shifts, buyer-intent signals, AI-tool adoption, awards, media features, Google ratings, local restaurant/phone-number call-outs, college events, first-time-in-role call-outs.

### 6.7 CTAs & Lead Magnets — 4 approaches
1. **The No-Brainer:** frictionless yes/no. "Mind if I send over a one-pager?" "Want me to share a quick teardown?"
2. **The Lead Magnet (give before you ask):** offer upfront value tied to their situation. Best late in a sequence and with skeptical buyers. Personalise it: "Given you just took over as Head of Sales at {{company}} and you're hiring SDRs, I put together a breakdown of how {{similar company}} built their outbound motion. Want me to send it over?"
3. **The Choice (autonomy + gamification):** "Take your pick - A) …, B) …, C) … Just reply with a letter." Frame it "A, B, or C - just reply with a letter and I'll shoot it over."
4. **The Disarming Question (remove all pressure):** "Am I even barking up the right tree?" "Total shot in the dark - worth a chat?" "Not sure if this is even relevant, but hey…" Anchor the doubt to a specific observation: "Saw you're shifting focus toward outbound - maybe I'm off, but could it be worth a chat?"

> **Also valid: no CTA at all.** Deliberately dropping the CTA can relax the message, act as a pattern disruptor, and help deliverability. Test it.

## 7. Before Hitting Send — 5-Step Checklist

Run every email through this filter:
1. **Ruthless clarity / brevity.** Strip to the core. Every extra word is friction. Cut any word a sentence works without. Clarity beats grammar — buyers skim, not study.
2. **Ease of reply.** How easy is it to say yes? Short 1–2 sentence paragraphs. Make the next step effortless and the message scannable.
3. **Pattern break.** Does it sound like everything else in their inbox? If yes, rewrite. Use language people don't typically use in cold outreach; ditch corporate speak.
4. **Self-interest check.** "If I were the decision-maker here, would I actually respond to this?" If not, rewrite — no matter how beautifully written.
5. **Cohesion & alignment.** Subject aligns with body; tone/voice/narrative consistent from opener to CTA (and across the sequence).

## 8. Quick-Reference Cheat Sheet (for fast generation)

- **Length:** ≤80 words. Paragraphs ≤2 lines. Subject ≤6 words.
- **Punctuation:** no em dashes, no exclamation marks, ≤1 question mark. No "Re:/Fwd:".
- **Never say:** impressive, innovative, cutting-edge, game-changing; "hope you're well," "just checking in," "circle back," "touch base," "leverage," "10x."
- **Don't:** name your company, state your mechanism, use ROI numbers, praise/flatter, use memes/GIFs.
- **Do:** front-load personalisation, lead with outcomes, use pattern disruptors, hedge with humbling disclaimers, keep tone neutral + human, offer low-friction CTAs (or none).
- **Pick 2–3 psychology levers** per message — don't cram all 13.
- **Follow-ups:** 2–3 max, each Different + Disarming + Direct, always a new angle.
- **Final gate:** "Would *I* reply to this?"
